Reading Google’s results

Open the test on Google’s site

When it’s done, here is what to look at:

  1. The top section, about what your real users are experiencing, comes from real shoppers on phones over the last 28 days. Where it’s there, it matters most. Smaller stores often don’t have it yet.
  2. Under “Diagnose performance issues”, the first circle, labelled Performance, is the lab score out of 100: 90 and above is good, under 50 is poor.
  3. Below the score, three numbers: Largest Contentful Paint: how long until the main photo or headline appears. Good is 2.5 seconds or less. Total Blocking Time: how long the page is too busy to answer a tap. Good is 200 milliseconds or less. Cumulative Layout Shift: how much the page jumps. Good is 0.1 or less.
  4. Further down, the insights and diagnostics marked with an estimated saving are where Google expects the biggest gains.

What the check measures

Real-shopper data
When a store has enough visitors, Chrome reports what they actually experienced on their phones over the last 28 days. It’s the truest measure of what your customers feel, so it comes first when Google has it.
Performance score
Google’s 0 to 100 summary of one test load on an emulated mid-range phone over a slow 4G connection. 90 and above is good; under 50 is poor. It’s useful for finding causes, and it moves a little from one run to the next.
Main image or headline on screen
Largest Contentful Paint: how long until the biggest thing on the screen, usually the hero or product photo, appears. Good is 2.5 seconds or less.
Time the page is frozen
Total Blocking Time: how long scripts keep the phone too busy to answer a tap while the page loads. Good is 200 milliseconds or less.
How much the page jumps
Cumulative Layout Shift: how far things move as late content pushes them around. Good is 0.1 or less.
Response to a tap
Interaction to Next Paint, from real shoppers only: how long the page takes to respond after a tap. Good is 200 milliseconds or less.
