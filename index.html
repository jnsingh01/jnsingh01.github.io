Scope, guarantees and ordering

A worked example

A common one, and a good illustration of how I think about this work.

A store with deeply detailed product pages hits the theme editor’s cap of 100 dynamic sources per template. The usual advice is to move the data into metaobjects and reference them from a list.metaobject_reference metafield on each product. That advice is right, and it is also a trap: the field will store up to 1024 references, but a Liquid for loop over it returns only the first 50, and {% paginate %} cannot be applied to a metafield value at all. Content past row 51 does not error. It simply is not there.

The fix is to shard the list — one reference metafield per section of the template rather than one for everything. Each list stays under the cap by construction, and the rendering collapses to the same few lines for every section:

{%- assign rows = product.metafields.custom[block.settings.field] -%}
{%- capture body -%}
  {%- for row in rows.value -%}
    {%- assign label  = row.label.value  | strip -%}
    {%- assign detail = row.detail.value | strip -%}
    {%- unless label == blank and detail == blank -%}
      <dt>{{ label }}</dt><dd>{{ detail }}</dd>
    {%- endunless -%}
  {%- endfor -%}
{%- endcapture -%}

{%- assign filled = body | strip -%}
{%- unless filled == blank -%}
  <section class="spec-group">
    <h2>{{ block.settings.heading }}</h2>
    <dl>{{ body }}</dl>
  </section>
{%- endunless -%}

If a product has no rows for a section, the capture comes out empty and the whole block — heading, wrapper and all — never renders. No conditional per feature, and nothing to maintain when a section is added next year.

That 50-reference limit is confirmed by Shopify staff on their developer forum rather than stated in the documentation, which is exactly why it catches people. Checking the thing that everyone assumes is the part of this job I enjoy most.
